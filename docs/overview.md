# What it is

North Bay Trails is a map for getting a feel for the trails of the North Bay, from the Russian River to Lake Hennessey, and for keeping track of which ones you've ridden. It isn't meant for turn-by-turn navigation. The goal is to make each park legible at a glance: where the trails are, how they connect, how they sit in the terrain, and which ones you still haven't ridden.

It's built to feel more like a picture book than a GIS tool. The region is shown as a long isometric diorama: a stretch of land from the Russian River to Lake Hennessey with its edges cut away, painted in a loose watercolor style. Grassy hills are golden, oak woodland and forest are loose green washes, the redwoods toward the coast are darker, water pools in soft blues, and little conifers dot the slopes. It's designed for a laptop screen.

## What's on the map

- **Seven main parks**, each with its own button and list section, painted in full color. Other parks and preserves, like Sugarloaf Ridge and Armstrong Redwoods, are painted in full color too. Land between them fades to a paler wash.
  - **Monte Rio Redwoods**, above the Russian River
  - **Taylor Mountain Regional Park**, on the southeast edge of Santa Rosa
  - **Trione-Annadel State Park**, on the east side of Santa Rosa, with neighboring Spring Lake and Howarth parks
  - **Jack London State Historic Park**, above Glen Ellen
  - **Hood Mountain Regional Park**, with its McCormick Ranch addition, above Kenwood
  - **The PUC forest**, Pacific Union College's land around Angwin
  - **Moore Creek Park**, above Lake Hennessey in Napa County
- **Trails**: every named trail and fire road in the region, in the parks or not, grouped so one trail is one thing you can click.
  - **Solid vermilion**: open to bikes, not ridden yet
  - **Solid teal**: ridden
  - **Dashed plum**: foot traffic only
  - **Green circle, blue square, black diamond** next to a trail's name: its difficulty rating, when OpenStreetMap has one
- **Lakes, the Russian River, peaks, and towns**, lettered by hand. More labels appear as you zoom in.

## What it's good for

- Seeing the whole region at once, then zooming into one park
- Ticking off trails as you ride them, and seeing what's left in each park
- Comparing trails by length and climbing before you pick one
- Checking at a glance whether bikes are allowed

## What it isn't

- **Not a navigation map.** Trail lines are generalized and the terrain is exaggerated for drama.
- **Not a conditions report.** Closures aren't shown. Annadel, for example, closes trails after rain.
- **Not the final word on access.** See [Where the data comes from](data.md) for how bike access is decided and where it's inferred. Always check with the land manager before you go.
