# Using the map

The screen has three main parts:

- **The map**, filling the window
- **The sidebar** on the left, with the park buttons, your ride progress, search, filters, and the trail list
- **The trail card** on the right, which slides in when you pick a trail

## Moving around

| To… | Mouse | Keyboard / buttons |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pan | Drag | |
| Zoom | Scroll (zooms toward the cursor) | `+` / `−`, or the **+** / **−** buttons |
| Spin the view | Right-drag | `Q` / `E` rotate 90°, or the ⟲ ⟳ buttons |
| Go back to the whole region | | `H`, or the ⌂ button |
| Hide the interface (for screenshots) | | `U` toggles it back and forth |

The compass above the buttons always points north, which helps after you've spun the view.

**Clean view.** Press `U` to hide every panel, button, and legend and leave just the painted map and its labels. That's handy for screenshots. Hovering doesn't highlight trails in this mode. A trail you've already selected stays highlighted; press `Esc` first if you don't want it. Press `U` again to bring everything back.

## Parks

The buttons under the title jump between parts of the map:

- **West** frames Monte Rio Redwoods and the Russian River, which sit on their own toward the coast.
- **East** frames the parks from Taylor Mountain to Angwin: Taylor Mountain, Annadel, Jack London, Hood Mountain, PUC, and Moore Creek. The list shows just those parks, grouped by park, and the progress bar counts just their trails.

- A park's button (**Monte Rio**, **Taylor Mountain**, **Annadel**, **Jack London**, **Hood Mountain**, **PUC**, or **Moore Creek**) flies the map to that park and narrows the trail list and the progress bar to it. The rest of the map stays visible, so you can still zoom out and pan around.
- **Everywhere** flies back to the whole region and lists every park's trails, grouped by park.

Picking a trail on the map while the list is narrowed to a different park switches the list to the trail's park.

## Tracking what you've ridden

- **Tick the box** to the left of a trail in the list, or click **Still to ride** on its card. The trail turns teal on the map, and the card shows the date you marked it. Click again to unmark it.
- **The progress bar** under the park buttons shows how many trails and miles you've ridden, for the selected park or for all of them.
- **To ride / Ridden** narrow both the map and the list. Use **To ride** to plan your next outing.

Your ridden list is saved in this browser only. It won't follow you to another browser or device on its own. Use **Export** at the bottom of the sidebar to download it as a file, and **Import** on another browser to merge it in. Importing never unmarks anything, and if both lists have a trail, the earlier date wins.

## Finding a trail

There are three ways to find a trail:

- **Point at it.** Hover over any line on the map to see its name, length, and whether you've ridden it. Click to select it.
- **Search.** Press `/` (or click the search box) and start typing. Results update as you type. Use the arrow keys to move through them and `Enter` to pick one.
- **Browse the list.** The sidebar lists every trail alphabetically, with its length and, when it's rated, a green circle, blue square, or black diamond. Hovering a list item highlights that trail on the map.

When you select a trail, the map flies to it and highlights it in gold. Everything else fades back a little. Press `Esc`, click an empty spot on the map, or close the card to deselect.

### Filters

The **All / Hike / Bike** buttons narrow both the map and the list:

- **Hike**: trails open to people on foot
- **Bike**: trails open to bikes, including trails where bikes are allowed on part of the route

## Reading a trail card

The card shows the following:

- **Park**, and a location hint when two trails in the same park share a name
- **Name**
- **Ridden or still to ride**, with the date you marked it
- **Who it's for**: hiking, mountain bikes, or no bikes. If bike access was inferred rather than stated in the data, the card says "likely allowed".
- **Miles, climbing, and descending**
- **An elevation profile** of the longest continuous stretch
- **Details**, when known: trail rating (green / blue / black / double black), technical grade (the S0–S6 mountain bike scale), hike difficulty, surface, who manages it, whether it's a fire road or a trail, and its elevation range

**Zoom to trail** re-frames the map on the selected trail.

## Sharing

Selecting a trail updates the page's address, for example `…/#trail=Lawndale%20Trail`. Picking a park or view does the same, with `…/#park=annadel` or `…/#park=east`. Copy the address and anyone who opens it lands on that trail or park. Your ridden list isn't part of the link.
